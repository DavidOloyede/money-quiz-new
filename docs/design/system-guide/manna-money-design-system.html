Product design · Reference guide

Manna Money Design System

Playful at the moments, calm at the money.

Version 1.0October 2026Web app and iPhone appOpen the Figma file

The rules, tokens and components behind Manna Money, written so anyone building a screen gets it right the first time. Every specimen here is live: press the buttons, type in the fields, answer the question of the day, and take the three-question quiz in section 2.6.

Four rules that explain most of the system

+10 XP$2,481.60
Playful at the moments, calm at the money.

The quiz, the question of the day, streaks and badges get color, bounce, sound and Omer. The dashboard, transactions, budgets and the Year Sheet stay quiet: clear numbers, no confetti, no alarms.

A darker bottom edge means you can press it.

Buttons, answer tiles and chips sit on a solid edge one shade darker than their face. Press one and it sinks. Cards and text stay flat, with a 1px hairline instead.

Honey is scarce on purpose.

About 60% cream and linen, 30% forest and sky, 10% honey. Honey means something was earned, so if everything is gold, nothing is.

AaAaAa
Three voices, each with one job.

Nunito is the coach (headlines, buttons, game text). Fraunces is the storyteller (scripture only). Inter is the ledger (money, tables, forms, body).

How to read the values. Everything comes from packages/core/theme.ts, src/index.css and docs/DESIGN.md, the single source both the website and the iPhone app read. Each value carries a tag:

Section 1

Visual language

Each color has one job and each typeface has one voice. Stick to the job and the app reads as calm even when it is playful. The warmth comes from the linen neutrals and the rounded type, not from extra color.

1.1Typography

Three families, all self-hosted from public/fonts/ as variable fonts, so there is no font service watching anyone download them. Each one is a voice with a single job.

Aa
The coach
Nunito · rounded sans · font-rounded

Headlines, buttons, quiz and game text, badges, celebration words. Looks best bold: 800 for headlines and buttons, 700 for game text, 900 for chips and scores.

Aa
The storyteller
Fraunces · softened serif · font-display font-soft

Scripture, the verse of the day and the "Why Manna?" story. Nowhere else. Italic, with the SOFT axis at 100 to round off its sharp corners.

Aa
The ledger
Inter · neutral sans · font-sans

Tables, amounts, money columns, forms and ordinary body text. Money always uses tabular-nums so columns of numbers line up.

1.4Voice

Warm, short, specific. Faith-informed, never preachy. Copy sets how loud a screen feels, so it belongs to the visual language too.

Instead of
Say
"Incorrect."
"Not quite. It was Dining, by $42."
"Great job!"
"Nice! You found your top expense."
"WARNING: Budget exceeded"
"$40 over on Dining this month. Want to adjust it?"
"Maximize your savings potential"
"A little set aside each week adds up."
"God wants you to budget"
"Enough for today, and a plan for tomorrow."
  • Name the actual thing: the category, the amount, the streak day.
  • One idea per message. If it needs a second sentence, the second one is the next step.
  • Words that welcome everyone: provision, steward, generosity, daily bread. Scripture lives in the verse of the day and quiz takeaways, not in buttons and errors.
Section 2

Component library

Every component is built the same way: a cream or colored face, a 1px hairline if it only holds content or a solid darker edge if it can be pressed, a radius from the scale, and type from one of the three voices. Learn the button and you know the answer tile, the chip and the icon tile.

2.1Buttons

Primary buttons press in. A 4px edge in a darker step of the face sits along the bottom; pressing drops the face 3px while the edge shrinks to 1px, so the button feels physical and nothing around it moves. The shared utilities are btn-primary, btn-secondary, btn-reward and btn-danger in src/index.css.

Section 3

Graphics system

One story, drawn two ways: manna falls from the logo and lands in Omer, the bowl. The mark, the mascot, the rewards and the confetti all follow the same few rules, so the art sits inside the interface instead of on top of it.

3.1The graphic language

Manna arrived every morning, enough for the day. So the logo is a bowl receiving three flakes, the mascot is that bowl with a face, and every reward is a flake falling into it. People who know the story will smile; everyone else just sees a friendly bowl.

Why the graphic language works

Round, never sharp

Round caps and joins, circles and pills everywhere. The drawing echoes Nunito, the coach's voice, so the art and the type feel like one hand.

Bold, clean outlines

Omer's outline is 2.5% of his rim width in linen-900. Icons use a 1.8 stroke on 24; the logo 1.6 on 32. Outlines keep the art legible at 48px.

Flat color, one cel shade

Palette colors only. Light comes from the top left; one shade, one step darker, sits on the lower right. No gradients on characters.

Manna is the particle

Rewards fall as flakes into the bowl, not coins or stars. Confetti is saved for milestones so it stays special.

Comic energy only at rewards

Halftone bursts, speed lines and one-word shouts ("NICE!", "+10 XP") appear only in moments, never on money screens.

Calm at the money

No characters on the dashboard, tables, budgets or imports. Money screens get icons and empty states at most.

3.2Logo mark

A forest tile, a honey bowl, and three flakes on their way in. Drawn on a 32-unit grid; it ships as public/manna.svg and the MannaLogo component. Hover any mark below: the flakes fall into the bowl.

  1. Tile. 32 × 32 units, 9-unit corner radius (28%), forest-700 #1C5A32.
  2. Outer flakes. Two honey-200 #F5DFA3 dots, r 1.6, at the top corners of the bowl's reach.
  3. Center flake. One honey-400 #E5B13C dot, r 2.1, lower and on the centerline: the one about to land.
  4. Bowl. A closed half-ellipse 19 units wide, 1.6 honey-400 stroke, round joins.
  5. Foot. A 9-unit line under the bowl, round caps.
  • Clear space: a quarter of the mark's width on every side.
  • Minimum size: 16px. Below 24px the hover motion is off.
  • Motion: on hover the flakes fall (1.5s loop, staggered) and the bowl dips half a unit. Reduce motion keeps it still.

3.3Omer model sheet

Omer is a round, honey-gold manna bowl with a friendly face, two stubby arms and two stubby legs. Cheerful and kind, not babyish. He is built on one unit: the rim width. Every other measurement is a fraction of it, so he stays on model at any size. New These drawings are the construction brief; the commissioned art is still in progress.

  1. Rim. 1 unit wide, an ellipse 0.2 tall. Cream with a honey-200 opening and a small heap of flakes peeking over.
  2. Bowl. 0.51 deep from rim line to base, honey-400 #E5B13C. Head and body are one shape.
  3. Shade. One honey-500 #CE9118 crescent on the lower right, plus a white gloss stroke on the upper left at 55%.
  4. Eyes. 0.14 × 0.18, centers 0.3 apart, on the eye line 0.41 down the bowl. Linen-900 with a white four-point star and a dot.
  5. Cheeks. Coral-300 #E9AEA0 ovals, 0.12 × 0.08, below and outside each eye.
  6. Mouth. 0.12 wide, centered, its top level with the eye bottoms.
  7. Arms. About 0.15 long: a 7px honey limb inside a 14px ink outline, round ends. No hands or fingers.
  8. Legs. Built exactly like the arms. Hips 0.15 apart under the bowl, about 0.16 showing, a tiny rounded foot turning outward. No knees, toes or shoes.
  9. Ground. A linen-900 shadow at 12%, 0.6 wide. It shrinks when Omer jumps.

Outline: linen-900 #201A13 at 2.5% of the rim width (4px at 200px, never under 2px). Red dashed lines are the centerline and eye line; blue dashes are the construction shapes.

Turnaround

Because the bowl is round, Omer turns cleanly: only the face and limbs move. In the side view the one visible arm hangs at the middle of the body, in front of the bowl and above the legs.

The eight poses

Expressions come from four controls: the eyes (open, happy, looking up), the mouth, the arms and the legs. The bowl itself is never redrawn.

3.5Light & icons

Every illustrated shape uses at most three flat values plus the ink outline. The shade is the same step as that hue's button edge, which is what ties the art to the interface.

  • Light comes from the top left, always. Shades sit lower right; the gloss stroke sits upper left. Buttons obey the same rule with their bottom edge.
  • Shade = the hue's edge step: honey-400 shades to honey-500, forest-600 to forest-800, coral to coral-700.
  • The darkest value is linen-900, never pure black, so the art never out-shouts the text beside it.
  • Skin and hair become theme tokens in theme.ts before any person is drawn.

Icon construction

Interface icons are line icons: a 24px grid, a 2px safe margin (the dashed keyline), a 1.8 stroke with round caps and joins, drawn in currentColor so they take the text color. The streak flame is the one filled, two-color glyph. Code

  • Sizes: 16 inside chips and feedback, 18–20 in buttons and card headers, 24 in navigation, 32 in the 64px icon tile.
  • Company logos are bundled, matched from Simple Icons or Plaid's own logo. Never hotlink a logo service: it would hand a third party every merchant the person pays.

4.2Corner radii

  • Three everyday shapes: cards 16 (rounded-2xl), buttons 12 (rounded-xl), chips fully round. The rest are for nesting.
  • Nesting rule: inner radius = outer radius − the padding between them, never below 4. A 16px card with a 4px inset holds 12px blocks.
  • Siblings match. Items in one row or list share a radius.

4.3Edges & shadows

Two kinds of shadow, never mixed. Press edges are hard and solid: they mean "you can tap this". Soft shadows are rare: they mean "this floats above the page". Resting cards have no shadow at all, just a 1px linen-200 edge. Press the live ones.

Section 5

Moments in motion

The still drawings in Section 3, moving. Every move starts and ends on its pose, so with motion off Omer simply stands in it. Tap any figure to replay it, and switch to Still to see the rest state that reduce motion shows.

5.1Omer’s moves

One move per pose and per outfit. In the app marks the moves the app already plays (Omer pops in, then breathes, hops or wobbles as one piece, from src/celebrate.css). Spec marks the part-by-part moves proposed here: an arm, a prop or the cape moves while the rest of him stays put.

The eight poses

The wardrobe

Each outfit’s move says what the moment is about: the Coach trains, the Wrangler rounds up, the Builder builds. The Detective now stands up straight, and the Coach’s sweatband and wristbands and the Wrangler’s bandana wear the app’s forest green.

  • Pivots are joints. Arms swing from the shoulder, props from the hand, hats from the rim, so nothing detaches at any frame.
  • Small amplitudes. Limbs turn 6–16°, the body squashes 3–6%. Bigger reads as frantic at 56px.
  • Rest on the pose. Every loop passes through the drawing in 3.3; reduce motion shows exactly that frame, with no particles.
  • A moment, not a screensaver. In the app, celebration loops run a few times and settle; only idle breathing keeps going.
